# Elemental Specimens

Every mark is a specimen of matter caught at one instant: a tongue of flame at the
moment it tears, a grain of sand in mid-fall, a single coil of a whirlwind. The work
treats these fleeting things the way a naturalist treats beetles pinned in a cabinet,
with the patient reverence of systematic observation. Nothing is decorative. Every
form exists because the substance it portrays would take exactly that form, and the
whole set is meticulously crafted so that a hundred copies, scattered and turning,
read as one living body rather than as countable stickers.

Form is born from the substance's own physics. Fire is a buoyant teardrop whose crown
frays into wisps; water is a tense skin around weight; stone is broken facets; wind is
a curve with no body at all, only the memory of motion. Silhouettes are drawn as
distance fields and then eroded by layered noise, so edges are never geometric and
never accidental: each one is the product of painstaking calibration between a pure
shape and the turbulence that wears it down.

Color is withheld. Every specimen is rendered in luminance alone, white on nothing,
so that the material's own palette, applied later as light, owns the hue entirely.
Value carries all the information: a hot core brighter than its rim, a facet lit
against its neighbour, the faint inner rings of a sound wave. This restraint is the
discipline that lets one sheet of marks serve fire and ice alike.

Scale and rhythm come from repetition with variation. A specimen is never alone: it
is a family of four or sixteen siblings, alike in grammar and different in detail,
the way no two snowflakes match. Animated families advance through their frames like
a time-lapse plate, from full presence to dissolution, each step drawn with the same
master-level care as the first.

Composition is centered, contained and breathing. Every specimen sits within its
cell with clear margins and fades to true transparency before the edge, so it can be
spun, stretched and overlapped without ever betraying a square. The contact sheet that
gathers them is a cabinet drawer: a quiet grid, thin clinical labels, a limited
palette, the evidence that something ephemeral can be studied, mapped and understood.
